# Worked examples

All source statements and identifiers below are synthetic illustrations, not claims about an actual owner's history.

## Apparent contradiction

**Input:** An assistant proposes “types are immutable essences.” The owner asks “would that follow?” A later owner passage says some kinds persist through changing causal processes. Today the owner clarifies that “stable” means persistent enough for inquiry. An old dossier calls the assistant proposal endorsed.

**Output:** Correct the dossier's attribution and keep a short provenance note because the mistaken endorsement circulated. The earlier source remains intact. The corpus can now explain stability without immutability when revision is authorized. It cannot describe an owner reversal from essentialism: the early turn was a question. Record the historical relationship as unresolved if other evidence is absent.

## Typology, abduction, institution and whitepaper

**Input:** A corpus has an account of kinds, an account of abductive inference, an institution's research program, and a whitepaper. A new argument explains why discovering kinds requires a particular form of abduction.

**Output:** If that relationship needs sustained development, give it an account such as “Discovering kinds,” linked from both subject homes. Otherwise place a section in the account whose question it chiefly answers and link from the other. The institution's program links to the relevant research argument; its operational commitments remain in the institutional home. The whitepaper may offer a distinct exposition or assemble existing accounts. Bind its edition, reconcile any original argument, and label inherited material as derivative so it cannot become independent corroboration for its own source.

No pairwise-intersection inventory is needed. Document count follows sustained questions.

## Expanding the corpus

**Input:** Add the owner's architectural thinking alongside an existing philosophy corpus.

**Output:** Reuse or create an architecture home with its own issue accounts and evidence. Add a short global route to both local homes. Link a genuine shared argument—perhaps classification in design—where it already lives, and record a real unresolved placement if necessary. Preserve the existing corpus's structure unless the authorized expansion exposes a dependency that needs reconciliation. Daily work begins at the relevant local home.

## Small edit and comprehensive harvest

**Input A:** “Sharpen this paragraph on warrant.”

**Output A:** Read the paragraph's supporting passage and relevant dependent account, revise the reasoning, and update an affected reference if needed. Ordinary version history is enough. A new search census or processing log would add no coverage guarantee to this task.

**Input B:** “Harvest all my conversations on warrant.”

**Output B:** Establish the archive/discovery boundary, screen candidates, read bounded sources/spans, checkpoint evidence and residuals, integrate into the warrant account, and state the achieved coverage with known gaps. A full keyword result does not become an all-relevant-conversations guarantee.

## A false completion claim

**Input:** The source contains a claim, reason, example defeating a weaker reading, and unanswered objection. The essay repeats only the claim. A register links the essay and says complete.

**Output:** Reopen the unit. Preserve the coherent argument in issue evidence, integrate the reason and interpretation-changing example where needed, and keep the objection visible as unresolved. If any substantive part is deliberately excluded, give the actual scope reason. A valid destination link is not evidence that the contribution survived.
