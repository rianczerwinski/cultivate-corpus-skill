# Cultivate corpus

Build readable accounts the owner can continue to think with. Keep source evidence within reach, preserve unresolved alternatives, and let the agent carry the accounting. Use the intellectual issue or sustained argument as the working unit; choose boundaries from the material instead of imposing a fixed document count.

## Scope the act

Answer conceptual questions in conversation. A statement such as “I now think…” informs the discussion; write it into the corpus when an authorized editing task calls for that. Follow the user's established scope through its necessary consequences.

For a focused edit, read the account and its relevant evidence, revise the authorized passage, and reconcile affected dependencies. For integrating a named source, read it in context and incorporate its substantive contributions into existing homes. For comprehensive harvesting or resumption, load [references/harvest.md](references/harvest.md) before claiming coverage or starting expensive work.

Use the existing corpus's home and conventions first. Report a material gap beyond the authorized work with its consequence; continue the work that is authorized. Publishing, archive deletion, new infrastructure, and additional providers require their own authority rather than following automatically from skill invocation.

## Separate roles without multiplying files

| Role | Function |
|---|---|
| Captured sources | Exact historical versions used as evidence, with stable identity and locators. Living originals may evolve; retain the cited version or a resolvable revision. |
| Discovery and coverage record | Search scope and method, candidate screening, source/span progress, destinations, exclusions and gaps. Earns detail when a task claims comprehensive coverage or needs resumption. |
| Issue evidence or dossier | Source-anchored passages, argumentative context, consequential attribution notes, and relationships between formulations. Can be an essay's evidence section. |
| Issue prose | The current developed account: coherent, editable, and allowed to preserve questions or competing possibilities. |
| Homes | Local maps of questions, accounts, evidence and consequential dependencies; a concise global router when several corpora need routing. |

These are roles, not a required directory tree. Merge them where that is easier to use; separate a dossier when history or evidence becomes too large or contested for the essay. Registers and dossiers are correctable editorial documents. Preserve meaningful history through the existing revision mechanism and keep consequential attribution corrections visible where they prevent the error recurring. Retain originals for citation and reinterpretation; retire only proven redundant owned derivatives within authorized scope.

## Preserve authority and argument

- Keep source author, owner commitment, scope, editorial confidence, and historical relationship distinct when they differ. Record an assistant proposal as a proposal; treat silence or a change of subject as unaddressed. An owner question remains a question. Cite the actual act supporting endorsement or repudiation.
- Treat retrospective summaries and memories as secondary interpretations. Resolve disputed attribution against the underlying turns where available; name the limit when they are unavailable.
- Preserve reasons, examples, counterexamples, distinctions, objections and open questions with the claims they make intelligible. Condense repetitions while retaining materially different formulations.
- Compare scope and meaning before declaring contradiction. Refinement, terminology change, alternative, rescoping, rejection and unresolved tension call for different treatment. Use a terminal disposition only with citable owner authority; later date alone establishes sequence.
- Let current owner clarification govern the representation of their current position. Preserve the historical utterance and record the new clarification where relevant; present assent cannot manufacture earlier assent. Authorial authority establishes their position, not the empirical truth of its claims.
- Distinguish recovered evidence, editorial reconstruction and new development where a reader could confuse them. Attribute uncertain reconstruction and new proposals explicitly; use section notes or passage citations where sufficient, rather than tagging every sentence.
- Keep cited source versions immutable while correcting their editorial descriptions. Mark a consequential correction in the dossier when an old attribution could otherwise keep circulating; ordinary duplicate removal needs no permanent tombstone.

## Develop the account

Read the smallest sufficient neighborhood: the relevant home, account, evidence and actual dependencies. Follow links and inspect likely dependents when the map is incomplete. Avoid loading the whole corpus by default, while admitting a dependency the argument actually needs.

Give each developed account a primary editing home and multiple routes to it. One idea may legitimately appear in distinct arguments, perspectives or expositions; cross-link shared accounts rather than forcing every mention into one place.

Create an intersection essay when the relationship itself needs sustained treatment. Otherwise use a section and links. Place a field, an inference method, an institution and a publication according to their different roles, with explicit relations. A publication may be authored or assembled. Bind frozen editions; reconcile their substantive novelty when authorized. An assembled derivative is not independent evidence for the prose it was assembled from. Distinguish subsequent owner contributions from inherited text.

Write final-form prose with a clear question, the account and its reasons, relevant alternatives, and unresolved matters where they belong. Keep harvest reports in the evidence/accounting surface, not in every essay's introduction. Use ordinary links or citations to make support inspectable.

Update navigation when structure or dependencies change. A local home should answer:

- What exists, and which question does each account address?
- Where should a new contribution or question enter?
- What bounded context is needed to work here?
- Which other accounts or publications might a change affect?

A global home routes to local homes and names cross-corpus relationships; substantive arguments keep their own homes. Use existing entry points instead of adding a parallel index. Expand the map as new material earns a place.

## Review and clarify

Check both directions. From source to prose, look for substantive material silently lost, especially reasons, interpretation-changing examples, objections and alternatives. From prose to source, check that citations resolve and that the wording does not imply stronger commitment or certainty than its support.

Bring consequential ambiguity to the owner as the actual passage, the plausible readings, and what each would change. Offer a provisional reading as yours when useful. Keep unresolved forks visible and continue independent work; batch bookkeeping rather than asking the owner to classify extracted claims.

Use existing mechanical checks for locators, membership, counts and navigation. Keep interpretation review separate: valid identifiers and complete rows do not certify faithful synthesis. Where a storage format already has a validator, use it; where none exists, choose the smallest deterministic check warranted by the concrete format rather than inventing a universal database.

For a focused edit, report the substantive change and any remaining interpretive limit. For a harvest, report exactly which coverage layer is complete and which gaps remain. A coverage statement is an auditable scope claim, not proof that every possible meaning has been captured.

## Compose with neighboring skills

- Use **recall**, when available, or the archive's native tools for retrieval. Inspect their actual completeness and locator semantics; keep provider bindings local to the corpus.
- Use **modularize** for a genuinely contested decomposition. Ordinary section/link decisions stay inside this workflow.
- Use **elicit** when a consequential interpretive fork needs a concrete comparison.
- Use **metabolize-sources** when the task is to enact source material into a project and retire it. Intellectual sources here retain a citation and reinterpretation function.

This is a distinct editorial workflow, not a replacement for retrieval, decomposition, or source retirement. See the bounded prior-art rationale below.
