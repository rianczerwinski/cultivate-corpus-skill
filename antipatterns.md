# Paired editorial traps

### Search completion becomes intellectual completeness

❌ Wrong: “All 60 keyword matches were read, so all your thinking is captured.”

✅ Right: “All 60 matching records were processed; related thinking outside those matches remains a discovery limit.”

Why it flips: query-result completeness and relevant-source coverage have different denominators.

### Saturation abandons included sources

❌ Wrong: Stop a comprehensive pass after two unproductive sources.

✅ Right: Finish the included set or report unfinished units; use saturation only to consider widening discovery.

Why it flips: a heuristic for exploration cannot discharge a bounded coverage promise.

### Repetition manufactures assent

❌ Wrong: An assistant's repeated formulation becomes “the owner's established view.”

✅ Right: Retain the assistant attribution and cite any actual owner endorsement; otherwise leave commitment unestablished.

Why it flips: circulation and silence supply no assent.

### Dates overwrite positions

❌ Wrong: Mark the older claim superseded solely because a newer formulation exists.

✅ Right: Compare scope and relationship, and cite owner authority for a terminal disposition; keep alternatives or tensions open otherwise.

Why it flips: chronology establishes sequence, not repudiation.

### Current clarification rewrites history

❌ Wrong: Relabel an earlier question as endorsement after the owner agrees today.

✅ Right: Use today's clarification for current prose while retaining the earlier question and its actual context.

Why it flips: current authority and historical evidence serve different purposes.

### A destination stands in for capture

❌ Wrong: Mark a span complete because its claim appears in an essay, although the reason and objection disappeared.

✅ Right: Reopen it, preserve the argumentative unit, and integrate or explicitly disposition substantive residuals.

Why it flips: links prove destination existence, not preservation of the argument.

### Dossiers become immutable clutter

❌ Wrong: Keep every editorial paraphrase and duplicate forever because sources must be preserved.

✅ Right: Preserve captured sources, sculpt editorial prose, and retain only meaningful lineage or correction notes that prevent recurring misinterpretation.

Why it flips: source fidelity does not require freezing the editor's work.

### Apparatus consumes the edit

❌ Wrong: A sentence revision triggers a new census, schema and changelog ceremony.

✅ Right: Verify its support and affected dependencies; use the existing revision mechanism.

Why it flips: a local edit makes no comprehensive-harvest claim.

### Mention becomes write permission

❌ Wrong: The owner says “I now think…” and the agent silently edits the corpus.

✅ Right: Engage the idea in conversation; integrate it when the active task authorizes editing.

Why it flips: substantive clarification and authorization to change files are distinct.

### Every overlap becomes a document

❌ Wrong: Create an essay for every pair of topics.

✅ Right: Give a sustained relational argument its own home; use sections and links for other overlaps.

Why it flips: documents follow coherent questions, not combinations of labels.

### Circular corroboration

❌ Wrong: An essay cites a whitepaper assembled verbatim from that essay as independent evidence.

✅ Right: Label the edition as derivative and trace support to the original sources; distinguish any subsequent original contribution.

Why it flips: another rendering is not another evidential witness.

### Bookkeeping becomes proof

❌ Wrong: “Every row and citation passes validation; interpretation is correct.”

✅ Right: Report structural checks separately from source-reading review, including its breadth and unresolved ambiguities.

Why it flips: mechanical consistency cannot establish faithful meaning.
